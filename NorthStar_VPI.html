Executive Guide: Vendor Performance Index

A management guide to the Vendor Performance Index. It argues that vendors should be judged as investments across the customer journey, not ranked on a single metric, and shows the budget decisions that judgment makes possible.

VPIWhich marketing investments deserve more capital?
01

The executive question

Most vendor scorecards ask which vendor is best. That is the wrong question.

Vendors are not interchangeable, and few are good at everything. The useful question is not who wins, but where each dollar should go: which vendors deserve more budget, which need fixing, and which should be replaced. VPI is built as an investment framework for answering that, not a leaderboard for ranking one.

02

Why this index exists

A single vendor metric hides more than it shows. Judge vendors on cost per lead and you reward the ones that generate cheap, low-intent volume. Judge them on click-through rate and you reward attention that never converts. Every one-number ranking optimizes for the wrong thing somewhere, because vendors specialize: some build awareness at the top of the funnel, others convert at the bottom, and a few are only worth their cost at one specific stage.

The business problem VPI solves is capital misallocation. Marketing budgets are finite, and spreading them by last quarter's lead count sends money to volume rather than value. VPI exists to allocate budget by where a vendor actually creates business, stage by stage, rather than by a single averaged score.

03

Design philosophy

The alternative to VPI is the ranked vendor table: sort by one KPI, spend more on the top rows. It fails because it treats a funnel as a point. A vendor that drives enormous awareness but rarely converts looks terrible on a lease-based ranking and excellent on an impression-based one, and both rankings are misleading on their own.

VPI takes the position that a vendor must be evaluated across the entire customer journey, then classified by what to do about it. Rather than asking which vendor is best, it separates vendors into scale candidates, optimization candidates, and elimination candidates. The design choice, in one line: score vendors along the funnel they actually work in, and turn the result into a budget decision, not a rank.

04

The framework

VPI reads a vendor across four stages of the customer journey. Each stage is a different job, and a vendor can be strong at one and weak at another.

Awareness

Whether the vendor reaches the right audience efficiently at the top of the funnel. Strength here means the vendor fills the pipeline; weakness means it is buying attention that never enters it.

Consideration

Whether that attention turns into genuine interest, visits and engagement rather than idle clicks. This is where cheap awareness is separated from valuable awareness.

Evaluation

Whether interested prospects become qualified leads at a defensible cost. A vendor that generates leads nobody can convert is failing this stage regardless of volume.

Conversion

Whether the vendor's leads actually sign. This is the bottom of the funnel, where a vendor either produces business or does not, and where the highest-value specialists earn their budget.

05

Executive interpretation

A score is only useful if everyone reads it the same way. Here is how to read this one.

A high scoremeans the vendor performs across the journey and converts spend into signed leases efficiently. It is a scale candidate: the case for moving more budget toward it is strong.
A low scoredoes not automatically mean cut. A low overall VPI on a vendor that is genuinely strong at one stage may be an optimization candidate, better used narrowly, rather than an elimination candidate.
A moving scorereveals specialization. A vendor rising on awareness but flat on conversion is telling an executive exactly what job it is good for, and what job it should not be paid to do.
06

Decisions this score enables

The point of the score is not the number. It is the decision the number makes possible.

Because VPI grades vendors by stage and classifies them by action, it turns a vendor list into a capital-allocation plan.

Budget reallocation

Move spend toward vendors that create business across the funnel and away from those buying volume that never converts, on evidence rather than habit.

Vendor optimization

Narrow a mixed vendor to the stage it is genuinely good at, capturing its strength without paying for its weakness.

Contract decisions

Enter renewal and negotiation with a clear, stage-level view of what each vendor is actually worth, rather than a single blended number.

Channel strategy

Balance the portfolio of channels by the jobs they do, ensuring awareness, consideration, and conversion are all adequately funded rather than over-indexed on one.

07

Common misinterpretations

A framework earns trust by naming its own limits. These are the readings to avoid.

  • A low VPI may indicate a strategic awareness campaign, not a failing vendor. A top-of-funnel partner that rarely converts directly can still be doing its job if awareness is the goal.
  • A high VPI is not permission to stop watching. Vendor performance shifts with market conditions and auction dynamics, and today's scale candidate can drift without notice.
  • VPI evaluates vendors, not the offer. When good vendors underperform everywhere at once, the problem is often the product, pricing, or availability, which VPI cannot see.
08

Relationship to PHI

VPI contributes roughly twenty-five percent of the Portfolio Health Index, the smallest of the three weights. Vendor performance is a lever on portfolio health rather than a direct measure of it: it shapes the pipeline that feeds GPI and OPI more than it moves occupancy on its own.

VPI should never be read in isolation, because a strong vendor mix cannot save a property with an operational problem, and a weak vendor mix will not sink a genuinely strong market overnight. VPI tells an executive where the marketing spend is working. GPI and OPI tell them whether it is enough.

Questions VPI answers
  • Which vendors deserve more budget?
  • Which vendors need optimization?
  • Which vendors should be replaced?
  • Where is each vendor strongest in the funnel?
