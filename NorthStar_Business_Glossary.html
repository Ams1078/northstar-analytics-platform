Business Glossary

The vocabulary of the NorthStar platform. These terms define the concepts the data model, measures, and dashboards assume. The Data Dictionary defines tables and columns; this glossary defines the business meaning behind them.

Business Perspectives Business Structure Business Processes Business Metrics Executive Indexes Platform Concepts

A term used without its qualifying context is ambiguous.

Business Perspectives

Several concepts in NorthStar are observed by more than one part of the business. Marketing, leasing, attribution, and operations record the same lifecycle event from different positions, so a single term can hold a different value in each. These are not different truths. They are different perspectives on one event, defined here and referenced throughout the glossary.

The lease lifecycle. Each stage is recorded by a different process.

Governing rule

A New Lease and a Move-In are separate events. A lease is signed on one date; occupancy begins on another. Marketing counts, signed leases, attributed leases, and move-ins are expected to differ.

New Lease

A signed lease agreement. The business observes it from three perspectives, which can carry different values for the same period.

Marketing View Marketing

Lease production credited to marketing activity within the funnel.

See also GPI, VPI, Cost per Lease

Leasing View Leasing

The authoritative signed lease agreement recorded in leasing. The basis for leasing performance and the reconciliation baseline.

See also Lead-to-Lease Rate, Move-In

Attribution View Attribution

A signed lease split by whether a tracked marketing journey earns credit: attributed or unattributed.

See also Attribution Coverage, Attribution

Lead

A prospect who has expressed interest in a property. Observed from two perspectives.

Marketing View Marketing

A lead with a known digital origin in a tracked channel or vendor.

See also Cost per Lead, Attribution Coverage

Leasing View Leasing

The complete lead count, including untracked sources such as referrals, walk-ins, and direct inquiries.

See also Lead-to-Lease Rate

Visit

A prospect engagement with a property. Marketing and leasing observe different events under this name.

Marketing View Marketing

A digital engagement with a property listing or page, between Click and Lead.

See also Cost per Visit

Leasing View Leasing

A physical or scheduled property tour, later in the funnel, with its own conversion to signed lease.

See also New Lease

Move-In Operations

The physical occupancy event that converts an available unit to occupied. It follows lease signing and may fall on a different date.

Business ContextAn operational event, distinct from the New Lease that precedes it.
See AlsoNew Lease (Leasing View), Scheduled Move-In, Occupancy

Scheduled Move-In Operations

A signed lease with a planned occupancy date that has not yet arrived. Represents committed incoming supply.

See AlsoMove-In, Move-In Coverage Ratio

Business Structure

The dimensions the portfolio is organized and reported by: where properties sit, and where marketing spend goes. Every fact rolls up through this structure.

Geography

Property

A single apartment community. The lowest level of the geographic hierarchy. The portfolio contains 120 properties totaling 35,753 units.

See AlsoMarket, Performance Tier

Market

A metropolitan area containing several properties. The level at which most competitive comparison occurs. The portfolio spans 12 markets.

See AlsoRegion, Market Strength

Region

The top of the geographic hierarchy, grouping markets into four areas: East, Central, West, and South.

See AlsoMarket

Unit

A single rentable apartment. The atomic element of capacity.

See AlsoOccupancy

Performance Tier

A property classification describing quality and expected behavior: Star, Good, Average, or Struggler.

Business ContextSets the benchmark a property is measured against. Strugglers do not appear in the strongest markets, nor Stars in the weakest.
See AlsoMarket Strength

Market Strength

A market classification of demand environment: Strong, Mid, or Weak. A separate axis from a property’s Performance Tier.

See AlsoPerformance Tier
Vendor & Channel

Channel

A category of marketing activity. NorthStar defines six: ILS, Paid Search, Paid Social, Display, Email, and Organic.

Business ContextA channel sets the performance envelope its vendors operate within. Organic carries no paid spend and captures walk-ins, search, and direct traffic.
See AlsoVendor, ILS

Vendor

A specific marketing platform within a channel, such as Zillow, Google Ads, or Meta. The portfolio works with 11 vendors across the six channels.

Business ContextThe same vendor performs differently across properties and markets, which is why the Vendor Performance Index scores vendors in context.
See AlsoChannel, VPI

ILS

An internet listing service where renters search for apartments, such as Zillow, Apartments.com, or Apartment List. Typically the highest-volume channel, priced by subscription.

See AlsoChannel

Business Processes

How a prospect moves from first exposure to a signed lease and an occupied unit: the marketing funnel, the leasing and occupancy events, and the attribution that connects spend to outcome.

Marketing Funnel

Marketing Funnel

The ordered sequence a prospect passes through: Impressions, Clicks, Visits, Leads, New Leases. Volume decreases at each stage.

Business ContextMarketing efficiency and vendor-quality measures derive from movement between stages.
See AlsoConversion Rate, New Lease

Impression

One display of a marketing asset to a potential renter. The top of the funnel and the broadest measure of reach.

See AlsoClick-Through Rate, Cost per Thousand

Click

A prospect action on a marketing asset that moves them toward a listing or landing page.

See AlsoClick-Through Rate, Visit

Conversion Rate

The proportion of prospects advancing from one funnel stage to the next. Each transition has its own rate.

Business ContextConversion rates isolate where a funnel succeeds or breaks, which volume figures do not show.
See AlsoClick-Through Rate, Lead-to-Lease Rate
Leasing & Occupancy

Occupancy

The proportion of a property’s units currently occupied.

Business ContextThe primary indicator of operational health. Read with lease pricing and Absorption.
See AlsoMove-In, Vacant Ready, Absorption

Move-Out

A resident vacating a unit, at lease end or early. Creates the vacancy leasing must refill.

See AlsoLease Expiration, Turnover

Vacant Ready

Unoccupied units prepared for immediate move-in. Distinct from vacant units still being turned.

Business ContextRevenue that leasing can capture now. A high count signals a leasing gap rather than an operational one.
See AlsoOccupancy, Absorption

Absorption

The rate at which available units are leased. Net Absorption is leases signed minus units vacated; Absorption Days estimates time to lease current vacant-ready inventory.

See AlsoVacant Ready, Occupancy

Lease Expiration

The scheduled end of a lease term. Each expiration is future vacancy risk. Tracked over a forward 60-day window.

See AlsoMove-In Coverage Ratio, Renewal

Move-In Coverage Ratio

Scheduled move-ins measured against lease expirations over the same forward window.

Business ContextAt or above 1.0, committed move-ins cover expirations. Below 1.0 signals a coming occupancy gap. Its marketing counterpart is Marketing Pipeline Coverage.
See AlsoLease Expiration, Marketing Pipeline Coverage

Turnover

The annual rate at which residents leave and units must be refilled. Higher turnover raises marketing and make-ready cost.

See AlsoMove-Out, Renewal

Renewal

An existing resident signing a new term rather than vacating. Retains an occupied unit and is not counted as a New Lease.

Business ContextRenewals reduce the vacancy leasing must refill. The platform models renewal rates by property tier.
See AlsoLease Expiration, Turnover
Attribution

Attribution

The assignment of credit for a conversion to the marketing touchpoints that contributed to it.

Business ContextBudget decisions depend on it. The same leases can favor different vendors depending on the model applied, which is why the platform compares several.
See AlsoAttribution Model, Prospect Journey

Prospect Journey

The full sequence of marketing touches a prospect experiences before converting or dropping off. The unit attribution operates on.

See AlsoTouchpoint, Attribution Model

Touchpoint

A single marketing interaction within a journey, attributed to a vendor. Touches are ordered.

See AlsoDirect Credit, Assisted Credit

Attribution Model

A rule for distributing credit across touchpoints. NorthStar supports six: First Touch, Last Touch, Linear, Time Decay, U-Shaped, W-Shaped.

Business ContextNo model is objectively correct. Each encodes a different theory of influence; the Attribution Lab compares them on the same data.
See AlsoDirect Credit, Attribution

Direct Credit

Credit assigned to the final touch before conversion. Each converted journey has exactly one.

See AlsoAssisted Credit

Assisted Credit

Credit assigned to earlier touches that contributed to but did not close the conversion.

See AlsoDirect Credit

Attribution Coverage

The share of actual leases carrying a tracked marketing journey.

Business ContextFull coverage is not expected. A range of 60 to 80 percent reflects the normal tracking gap; the remainder is real leasing activity.
See AlsoNew Lease (Attribution View)

Business Metrics

The rates and costs derived from the funnel and leasing events. Several carry multiple forms; the reporting context determines which applies.

Click-Through Rate

The share of impressions that result in a click.

Business ContextClicks ÷ Impressions
See AlsoImpression

Lead-to-Lease Rate

The share of leads that become signed leases. Because Lead and New Lease each hold multiple forms, this is a family of rates; numerator and denominator must share the same form.

Business ContextValid pairings: Marketing over Marketing, or Actual over Actual. Read with Cost per Lease and Occupancy.
See AlsoLead, New Lease

Cost per Lead

Marketing spend divided by leads produced.

Business ContextMarketing Spend ÷ Leads Interpret with lead quality: a low figure with weak Lead-to-Lease produces expensive leases.
See AlsoCost per Lease

Cost per Lease

Marketing spend divided by the approved lease count for the reporting context. The denominator’s form must be stated.

Business ContextCost per Lease (Actual) uses Actual New Leases. Cost per Lease (Vendor) measures a vendor’s spend against its attributed leases. In some scoring measures, CPL abbreviates cost per lease, not cost per lead.
See AlsoCost per Lead, Lead-to-Lease Rate

Cost per Visit

Marketing spend divided by listing visits.

Business ContextMarketing Spend ÷ Visits
See AlsoVisit

Cost per Thousand

Marketing spend per thousand impressions. The standard top-of-funnel pricing measure.

Business Context(Marketing Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000
See AlsoImpression, Click-Through Rate

Marketing Pipeline Coverage

Funnel lease pace measured against upcoming lease expirations over the same forward window. The marketing counterpart to Move-In Coverage Ratio.

Business ContextWhere Move-In Coverage measures committed supply, this measures projected demand. The gap between them is a sustainability signal.
See AlsoMove-In Coverage Ratio

Executive Indexes

The four composite scores that condense the platform’s measures into figures an executive can act on. Each combines several measures on a common scale and supports a specific decision. Construction is documented in the Executive Index Framework.

Composite Index

A single score built from several weighted, normalized measures. Makes properties, markets, and vendors comparable despite differing size.

Business ContextReduces many measures to one number while preserving drill-down. The design choice is which measures to include and how to weight them.
See AlsoGPI, OPI, VPI, PHI

Geographic Performance Index

A composite ranking geographies on marketing performance, combining outcome, influence, and cost efficiency, weighting results above spend.

Business ContextSupports deciding where marketing performs well and where it needs attention.
See AlsoPHI

Operations Performance Index

A composite describing operations health, split into a current-state and a future-state score.

Business ContextKeeping both visible shows whether a property healthy now is trending toward difficulty.
See AlsoOccupancy, PHI

Vendor Performance Index

A composite ranking vendors on how effectively they move prospects through the funnel, weighting conversion most heavily and judging vendors against the field.

Business ContextSupports deciding which vendors to invest in, optimize, or cut.
See AlsoVendor, PHI

Portfolio Health Index

The executive summary score for the portfolio, combining the geographic, operations, and vendor indexes.

Business ContextGPI × 0.45 + OPI × 0.30 + VPI × 0.25 Weights geography most heavily as the spine of marketing performance.
See AlsoGPI, OPI, VPI

Platform Concepts

Terms required to read the platform’s data correctly. These are not business vocabulary; they describe how the data is produced and governed.

DataSource

A tag on every fact record identifying which process produced it. DataSource 1 is the canonical truth; 2, 3, and 4 are pipeline reconstructions.

Business ContextA query not filtered on DataSource averages truth with reconstructions and returns a meaningless figure. All reporting uses DataSource 1.
See AlsoCanonical Truth, Reconciliation

Canonical Truth

The authoritative data written directly by the generator as DataSource 1. The answer key for pipeline reconstructions.

See AlsoDataSource, Reconciliation

Reconciliation

The comparison of a pipeline’s reconstructed facts against canonical truth, producing a measured variance.

See AlsoDataSource, Canonical Truth

Semantic Model

The governed layer between warehouse and reports, holding relationships, reusable measures, and scoring logic.

Business ContextBusiness definitions live here once, so every report reads the same figure.
See AlsoMeasure, Grain

Measure

A defined calculation in the semantic model, computed at query time. The Measure Catalog documents each.

See AlsoSemantic Model

Grain

The level of detail one record represents, such as one row per property per day. Determines what a table can answer.

See AlsoMeasure
Related documentation

The Data Dictionary defines the tables and columns these terms are stored in. The Measure Catalog defines their calculations. The Executive Index Framework documents how the four indexes are constructed.
