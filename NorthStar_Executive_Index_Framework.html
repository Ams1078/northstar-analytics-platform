Executive Decision Framework

NorthStar organizes hundreds of operational metrics into four executive indexes, each measuring an independent dimension of portfolio health. This is the theory behind that decision, and the reasoning that shaped every score in the platform.

01

Why executive indexes exist

Modern organizations rarely suffer from a lack of data. They suffer from a lack of clarity.

A regional manager may have hundreds of metrics within reach: occupancy, cost per lead, marketing spend, conversion rate, lease velocity, traffic, retention, vendor performance. Each answers one narrow question. None answers the larger one that a leader actually needs settled: how healthy is this part of the business?

NorthStar was built to close that gap. It does not replace operational metrics. It organizes them into four executive indexes, each measuring an independent dimension of performance. Together they form a complete picture of portfolio health.

The result is not a dashboard. It is a management framework.

02

Four indexes, four questions

Each index exists to answer one executive question that no single metric can. None of what follows is about how the score is computed. It is about what the score is for.

GPIGeographic Performance Index

QuestionAre our markets performing as expected?

Marketing should never be judged on volume alone. High leasing volume in an already strong market can hide inefficient spending, while a struggling market may be quietly outperforming expectations. GPI evaluates a geography against its peers, balancing market reality, marketing influence, and marketing efficiency.

The goal is not to measure activity. It is to measure competitive geographic performance.

OPIOperations Performance Index

QuestionCan operations sustain future demand?

Traditional operational reporting looks at current occupancy and stops there. OPI separates current health from future risk, combining operational reality with marketing support to judge whether a property that is healthy today will still be healthy sixty days from now.

Its purpose is early intervention, not historical reporting.

Current healthFuture risk
VPIVendor Performance Index

QuestionWhich marketing investments deserve more capital?

Vendors rarely fail at every stage. Some build awareness, others convert, and some perform only at the bottom of the funnel. VPI evaluates vendors across the full customer journey to separate scale candidates from optimization candidates and elimination candidates.

It exists to guide budget allocation, not simply to rank vendors.

ScaleOptimizeEliminate
PHIPortfolio Health Index

QuestionIs the business healthy?

Executives rarely have time to interpret dozens of operational reports. PHI synthesizes the three performance domains into a single indicator. It does not replace operational detail; it prioritizes where an executive should investigate first.

PHI is a navigation tool, not a replacement for analysis.

03

Why indexes, not KPIs

KPIs measure individual events. Indexes measure systems.

A KPI reports a fact. An index renders a judgment. The same subject, asked two different questions, produces two very different kinds of answer.

OccupancyAre units occupied?
→
OPIIs this property's operational model sustainable?
Marketing SpendHow much did we spend?
→
GPIWas the investment effective relative to its peers?
Vendor CTRDid users click?
→
VPIDoes this vendor create business value?

That difference, the move from event to system, is the entire philosophy.

04

Why not just PHI?

If one score is too blunt, it is fair to ask the opposite question. Why not stop at PHI, the single number executives actually want?

Because a single score tells an executive where to investigate. It does not tell them why performance changed. A portfolio health number that drops is a prompt, not an explanation, and an organization that manages to one number quickly loses the ability to say who is responsible for it.

NorthStar intentionally separates performance into three independent domains before synthesizing them into PHI. That separation preserves accountability. Marketing owns GPI. Operations owns OPI. Vendor strategy owns VPI. PHI coordinates them; it does not replace them. The composite exists to point, and the three indexes beneath it exist to answer.

05

06

Independent by design

The four indexes are intentionally independent. Each measures one business discipline, and no index reaches into another's domain. GPI never reads operations. OPI never scores vendors. Only PHI integrates the three, and it does so by weight, not by blending their internals.

That independence is a governance decision, not an accident of the model. It keeps each index interpretable on its own, and it means a change in one discipline cannot silently distort another. An operations problem shows up in OPI, where an operator will look for it, and nowhere it does not belong.

What NorthStar measures

NorthStar does not measure marketing. It does not measure operations. It does not measure vendors. It measures the health of a residential real estate portfolio through those disciplines.

That distinction defines the platform.
