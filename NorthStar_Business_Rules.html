Business Rules

How the concepts defined in the Business Glossary are counted and governed. The glossary answers what a term means. These rules answer when it is counted, which date governs it, what is excluded, how it aggregates, and what it must reconcile to. Where a term is defined in the glossary, it is not redefined here.

A metric without stated counting rules is not comparable across reports.

Counting Inclusion / Exclusion Date Attribution Aggregation Reconciliation Data Quality
Rule 0 · Governing constraint

Every rule below assumes DataSource = 1. All fact tables carry canonical truth (DataSource 1) alongside pipeline reconstructions (2, 3, 4). Any measure not filtered to DataSource 1 blends truth with reconstructions and is not valid for reporting. This constraint precedes every counting rule and is never waived.

1. Counting Rules

What qualifies as one of each counted event. Counting rules define the condition a record must satisfy to increment a metric.

Actual New Lease

Glossary: the authoritative signed lease recorded in leasing.

Counts when
A new resident signs a lease for a previously unoccupied or turning unit, recorded in the leasing fact at DataSource 1.
Grain
Property × Lease × Date. One row per signed lease.
Excludes
  • Renewals of an existing resident
  • Cancellations reversed before the reporting period
  • Transfers between units
  • Scheduled move-ins not yet realized as signed leases
Reporting date
Lease signed date (DateKey on the leasing fact).

Marketing New Lease

Glossary: lease production credited to marketing activity within the funnel.

Counts when
A funnel conversion is recorded against a property, channel, and vendor for the activity date.
Grain
Property × Vendor × Channel × Date.
Relationship to Actual
A marketing count, independent of the leasing transaction. It need not equal Actual New Lease for the same period; the difference is expected and reconciled, not corrected.

Move-In

Glossary: the physical occupancy event converting an available unit to occupied.

Counts when
A resident takes occupancy of a unit. Recorded in the operations fact, separate from the lease-signing event.
Grain
Property × Date.
Excludes
  • Signed leases with a future occupancy date (see Scheduled Move-In)
  • Renewals, which retain occupancy without a move event
Reporting date
Actual move-in date, which may differ from the lease signed date.

Lead (both forms)

Glossary: a prospect expressing interest. Marketing View is tracked; Leasing View is total.

Marketing Lead counts when
A prospect action carries a tracked digital origin in a channel or vendor.
Actual Lead counts when
Any lead is recorded, including untracked walk-ins, referrals, and direct inquiries.
Rule
The two are never summed. A metric states which form it counts, and a conversion rate uses one form consistently in numerator and denominator.

2. Inclusion / Exclusion Rules

What is intentionally counted and what is intentionally ignored. Exclusions are deliberate and documented, so an absent record is understood as a rule rather than a gap.

Occupancy

Glossary: the proportion of a property's units currently occupied.

Formula
Occupied Units ÷ (Occupied + Vacant)
Includes
  • Occupied residential units in the active portfolio
Excludes
  • Units flagged offline or unavailable for lease
  • Any inventory outside the property's counted unit total
Note
The synthetic portfolio models only rentable residential units. Model, maintenance, and offline categories are named here for governance completeness; when present in a real deployment they are excluded from both numerator and denominator.

Attribution Coverage

Glossary: the share of actual leases carrying a tracked marketing journey.

Includes
  • Actual New Leases with at least one qualifying tracked touch inside the attribution window
Excludes
  • Leases with no tracked journey (walk-ins, referrals, direct)
  • Organic touches, which never receive attribution credit
Expected range
60 to 80 percent. Values outside this range are a data-quality signal, not a target to close.

Renewal

Glossary: an existing resident signing a new term rather than vacating.

Included in
  • Retention and turnover calculations
  • Move-in coverage as reduced expiration risk
Excluded from
  • New Lease counts (all forms)
  • Marketing acquisition and cost-per-lease measures
Rate basis
Modeled by property tier. Higher-tier properties renew at higher rates.

3. Date Rules

Which date governs each metric. Because a single lease touches several dates, the governing date determines which reporting period a record falls into. Using the wrong date shifts events between periods.

Governing date by metric

Actual New Lease
Lease signed date.
Marketing New Lease
Marketing activity date of the crediting touch.
Move-In
Actual occupancy date, independent of the lease signed date.
Scheduled Move-In
Planned occupancy date, always in the future relative to the snapshot.
Occupancy
End-of-day snapshot on the as-of date. A point-in-time state, not a period sum.
Lease Expiration
Scheduled term-end date. Forward windows count expirations dated within the next 60 days of the snapshot.
Attribution
Anchored to the lease conversion date; touches count only if within 7 days before it.

4. Attribution Rules

How credit for a converted lease is assigned across marketing touches. These rules are locked; the Attribution Lab compares models but does not alter the governing constraints below.

Credit assignment

Glossary: Attribution, Attribution Model, Direct / Assisted Credit.

Window
7 days. Only touches within 7 days before the conversion are eligible for credit. The window is locked.
Credit sum
Credits across a converted journey sum to exactly 1.0. No conversion distributes more or less than one whole credit.
Direct credit
Exactly one touch per converted journey receives direct credit: the final eligible touch before conversion.
Organic
Organic touches never receive direct credit and are flagged distinctly. Organic captures untracked and direct traffic, which carries no vendor to credit.
Default model
Time Decay, weighting e^(-λ × DaysBeforeLease), normalized to 1.0. Other models reweight the same eligible touches and never change eligibility or the credit-sum rule.

5. Aggregation Rules

How metrics roll up across the geographic hierarchy. Aggregation method is part of a metric's definition: a rate and a snapshot do not aggregate the same way as a count.

Aggregation method by metric type

Counts
Sum across the grouping. New Leases, Leads, Move-Ins, and Expirations sum from property up to market and region.
Occupancy
Recomputed at each level from summed Occupied and Vacant units, not averaged from property percentages. A weighted result by unit count, so large properties carry proportional weight.
Snapshots
Occupancy, Vacant Ready, and Scheduled Move-Ins aggregate as end-of-day values on the as-of date, summed across properties, never across dates.
Rates and costs
Recomputed from aggregated numerator and denominator at the target level. Never averaged from lower-level rates, which would misweight.
Indexes
Composite scores are computed at the level they describe. A market GPI is not the mean of its property GPIs.

6. Reconciliation Rules

What must agree with what. Reconciliation turns pipeline correctness into a measured variance rather than an assumption. Canonical truth is the baseline; reconstructions are measured against it.

Reconciliation baselines

Glossary: DataSource, Canonical Truth, Reconciliation.

Baseline
DataSource 1 (canonical truth) is authoritative. DataSources 2, 3, and 4 are the CRM, spend, and operations pipeline reconstructions measured against it.
Funnel to spend
Every funnel row must match a spend key for the same property, vendor, and date. Unmatched funnel rows are a reconciliation failure.
Attributed to actual
Attributed New Leases must not exceed Actual New Leases for any period. Attributed plus Unattributed equals Actual.
Marketing to leasing
Marketing New Lease and Actual New Lease are reconciled, not forced equal. Their variance is reported; it is expected to be non-zero.
Publish rule
A canonical publish deletes only the run date at DataSource 1 before reinserting, never touching other DataSources or dates.

7. Data Quality Rules

The tolerances a valid dataset is expected to hold. A value outside tolerance is a signal to investigate the pipeline, not to adjust the metric.

Expected tolerances

Occupancy bound
Occupied Units never exceed a property's total units. Vacant never falls below zero. Both are hard-enforced at generation.
Attribution coverage
Expected 60 to 80 percent. Sustained values outside this range indicate a tracking or join problem.
Turnover realism
Annual turnover expected in the 45 to 55 percent range for operational realism. Large deviations indicate a calibration issue.
Credit integrity
Attribution credits per journey sum to 1.0 within floating-point tolerance. Any journey summing outside 1.0 is rejected.
Date coverage
Fact dates are gapless across the reporting horizon. A missing date is a pipeline failure, not an empty day.
